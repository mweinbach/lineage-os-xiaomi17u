# From Black Screen to TWRP

## Style prompt

A warm, playful animated workshop, presented as an illustrated engineering
story rather than a screen recording. Two original characters talk to each
other: Nezha is a curious orange phone with expressive eyes, arms and sneakers;
Patch is a small teal robot engineer with a cream face, antenna and work apron.
Their eyes blink, bodies settle and hands gesture while their mouths respond
to the actual dialogue audio. Big readable diagrams show one idea at a time.
The visual contrast is between friendly cartoon personalities and precise
technical labels. Use landscape 1920 by 1080 at 30 frames per second.

## Colors

- Warm paper `#F6F0E5`: persistent workshop canvas and light surfaces.
- Ink `#20343C`: outlines, type and the deepest shadows.
- Teal `#187568`: Patch, recovery success and diagram accents.
- Coral `#EC7955`: Nezha, emphasis and the early failure chapter.
- Amber `#EAB850`: lamp light, tools and tiny decorative details.

## Typography

Use Bricolage Grotesque for statements and conversational captions, with
800-weight headlines and 400-weight supporting text. IBM Plex Mono is the
quiet voice for file names, code, counters and evidence labels. At 1080p,
headlines are 68–92 px and captions are 42–50 px. Keep all important content
inside an 80 px safe margin; reserve the bottom caption rail separately from
the character faces and diagram area.

## Motion

Use visible character acting, not still portrait slides: speech-responsive
mouths, occasional blinks, listening tilts, hand gestures and subtle breathing.
Use smooth paper-panel reveals between related scenes, a circle reveal for
the working recovery, and a gentle final hold. Every scene has distinct
entrances; keep its outgoing content intact until the transition. Build all
animation synchronously and make it seekable. No flashing or strobing.

## What not to do

- No real-person likeness, cloned voice or synthetic device footage claim.
- No serials, private logs, credentials, keys or passwords on screen.
- No invented benchmark, completed-ROM claim or stock/OEM trust claim.
- No generic neon dashboard, dense code wall, gradients in headlines or
  repeated grids of identical cards.
- No narration-only slideshow: both characters must visibly talk and react.

## Production choices

English dialogue, approximately two minutes, two distinct local synthetic
voices, readable embedded captions, and an editable Hyperframes composition.
HeyGen's connected service required reauthentication, so this production uses
local animation and local speech synthesis. No phone access is required.
