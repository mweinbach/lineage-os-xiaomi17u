## What actually records

| Setting | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1080p30 HEVC | Records and plays, 24 fps in the dark (low-light auto frame rate) |
| 4K60 Dolby Vision | Records and plays, HEVC Dolby Vision profile, live audio, no dropped frame |
| 8K30 | File writes but the video track is not decodable and is not indexed; unusable as delivered |
| 4K120 | The app aborts in the muxer with a write overflow; no file |
| 4K60 sustained (10 min) | Same muxer abort about 3 seconds in; no file, so no thermal curve |

The 4K60 clip reports about 30 fps in the container because Dolby Vision writes
a 30 fps base layer. The 8K failure and both crashes share a cause on the
encoder path: the Xiaomi recorder asks the encoder for length-prefixed HEVC
units (`vendor.qti-ext-enc-nal-length-bs`), and this platform's `MPEG4Writer`
either cannot convert them (8K, no decodable track) or aborts writing them
(4K120 and sustained 4K60, a fortify write overflow that kills the app). The
usable set today is up to 4K60; 8K, 120 fps and long 4K60 runs need the
QTI-patched media writer that the stock system carries and this build does not.

## Microphone

A recording was made while the phone played a three-second test tone through
its own speaker starting five seconds in. The recorded audio was silent before
the tone, peaked near full scale during it, and fell afterward, in both Dolby
Vision and plain HEVC clips. The microphone captures live room audio and is not
a dead or looped track.

## CameraOpt methods on real events

`dumpsys cameraopt` after camera use counts the ported methods being called:
`reclaimMemoryForCamera` and `boostCameraByThreshold` each ran, and the reclaim
policy fired three scenes with five kills. Entering 4K video raised the
`miuicam_video_switch_event` scene, which killed only cached background Google
apps above the service-B adjustment floor (adj 905 to 955) and wrote the cgroup
reclaim once. Visible, foreground and persistent processes were never touched.
This is the ported factory behavior running against the real Xiaomi camera.

## Leica: what is on and what is locked

The cloud Leica color filters are enabled. The app's cloud config database
carries the Leica filter module with the six Leica LUT looks (Vibrant, Natural,
Monochrome, Monochrome HC, Sepia, Blue), and the Video top bar shows "Leica
Vibrant". These are delivered over Xiaomi's cloud filter service and work here.

The Leica M3 and M9 "Leica Essential" film looks are a separate feature and are
locked to the Leica Edition hardware. The mode's own support check requires the
device to be the Leica-edition class and `ro.theme_customize` to equal `LCC`.
That property comes from a per-hardware ODM file that exists only on the Leica
Edition; this unit is the standard Xiaomi 17 Ultra (`2512BPNDAC`), not the
Leica Edition (`25128PNA1C`, "Xiaomi 17 Ultra by Leica"), so the property is
empty. Forcing `ro.theme_customize=LCC` to test the gate made the stock camera
app run its cloud security and native attestation check, report
`security_check_fail_cloud`, and close itself after three seconds. The M3/M9
looks cannot be unlocked by a property flip on standard-edition hardware. The
forced property was cleared by a reboot; the camera app then launched normally.

## What this does not prove

per-lens focus, stabilization and effect quality. The lens and effect
comparisons still need a lit scene and a person. The
[structured record](../research/tier2-camera-v16-20260910.json) holds the
numbers; media, logs and the decoded proprietary cloud tables stay under the
ignored evidence directory.
